Purpose & scope

Estimate diaphragm vessel volume from fluid expansion, pressures and initial water reserve.

Ideal-gas model; no product selection or whole-building fill-pressure calculation. bar(g) is converted to bar(abs) internally.

Inputs

Use drawings, design conditions and manufacturer data. Units and example values follow the calculator; example values are not universal design requirements.

ParameterValue / interpretation

Volume allowance · %Allowance applied to the result; not a substitute for entering all losses.

Steps

  1. Enter total system volume and cold/hot temperatures; select water or documented fluid expansion.
  2. Enter pressures at vessel elevation: pre-charge, cold fill, maximum operating and atmospheric.
  3. Enter reserve/allowance; read minimum volume and verify the actual vessel, relief valve and pressure/temperature ratings.

Method & limitations

Ideal-gas model; no product selection or whole-building fill-pressure calculation. bar(g) is converted to bar(abs) internally.

Use absolute pressures: p = gauge pressure + atmospheric pressure. With isothermal ideal gas, incremental acceptance = p₀·(1/p cold − 1/p max). Initial water fraction = 1 − p₀/p cold.

Volume = max(expansion / incremental acceptance × allowance, reserve / initial water fraction). The vessel must accept the expansion and hold the initial reserve. Pure-water temperatures are limited to 4–90°C; use manufacturer expansion data for glycol or other fluids.

Pre-charge ≤ cold fill < maximum pressure. Maximum is the allowed operating pressure, not the relief setpoint without its operating allowance. Verify static pressure, pump location and vessel pressure/temperature limits. Not a pump-cycling accumulator calculation.

Common mistakes

Entering absolute pressure into gauge fields; confusing system/vessel volume; excessive pre-charge; requesting a reserve when pre-charge equals cold fill.
