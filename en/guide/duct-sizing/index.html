Purpose & scope

Size or check round/rectangular ducts against airflow, velocity and friction-loss rate.

Straight-duct losses only. Hydraulic diameter is used for friction; equivalent round diameter is for comparison. At most 10 candidates are retained.

Inputs

Use drawings, design conditions and manufacturer data. Units and example values follow the calculator; example values are not universal design requirements.

ParameterValue / interpretation
Design presetSelect the material/system appropriate to the actual application; verify reference values.

Steps

  1. Use Size selection for candidates or Check duct for an existing section.
  2. Enter airflow, units, material, temperature and project design limits.
  3. Read velocity, Pa/m and section loss; choose a table row and save it in the project list.

Method & limitations

Straight-duct losses only. Hydraulic diameter is used for friction; equivalent round diameter is for comparison. At most 10 candidates are retained.

V = Q/A; Dh = 4A/P; Re = ρVDh/μ. Friction loss R = fρV²/(2Dh). The Darcy friction factor is solved iteratively with Colebrook for turbulent flow. Laminar flow uses f = 64/Re (an approximation for rectangular ducts); Re between 2,300 and 4,000 triggers a transitional-flow warning.

For rectangular ducts, De = 1.30(ab)^0.625/(a+b)^0.25. De is for equivalent round duct comparison only; friction calculations use hydraulic diameter and actual velocity. Air density follows the ideal gas law and viscosity follows Sutherland’s law. Up to 10 qualifying sizes closest to the maximum friction loss are retained. Dimensions range from 100 to 2,000 mm in 50 mm increments, with an aspect ratio no greater than 4:1.

Velocity and friction limits are user-defined design inputs, not universal standards. The standard and smoke extract presets reproduce the supplied sample settings. Results cover straight duct friction and exclude elbows, tees, terminals, equipment and other local losses. .

Common mistakes

Confusing CFM and m³/h; treating Pa/m as total pressure loss; summing parallel branches; assuming a recommended size verifies noise or outlet velocity.

Projects & reports

  1. Choose or create an engineering project and enter your actual design inputs.
  2. Project inputs/results are autosaved in this browser. Where a saved-list panel is available, name and add the selected section or alternative to compare multiple options.
  3. Review the report before exporting PDF. In the print dialog select Save as PDF. Export JSON for backup and use Open file to restore it; PDF cannot restore project data.

Saved results do not automatically recalculate when another tool changes. Review any dependency warning, recalculate and update the affected results.
